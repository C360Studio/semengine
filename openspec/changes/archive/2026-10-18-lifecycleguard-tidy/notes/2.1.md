Choices the design does not spell out:

- D1's six rows share one failure-line shape, `"%s = %v, %s"`: the call (`Start(nil)`, `Stop(cancelled)`, ...), the
  error, and the row's `want` text. Each row's `want` is the design's line with its `%v` filled in, so the output
  carries D1's lines verbatim, including the M1 and M4 texts.
- The ended rows match the sentinel (`context.Canceled`, `context.DeadlineExceeded`) rather than `ctx.Err()`, so the
  oracle does not come from the context under test. The expired context is `context.WithDeadline` at `time.Unix(0,
  0)`, past on construction; nothing waits on a clock.
- In a `Start` row the counted function is `start`; in a `Stop` row it is the cleanup, handed to both the setup
  `Start` and every `Stop`, as D3 does. "Guard still unused" after a refused `Start` is read from the next live
  `Start` being admitted, not from the guard's fields.
- The message check replaces `Start` with `Stop` in `Start(nil)`'s message and compares it with `Stop(nil)`'s.
- In D3 the cleanup counts with `atomic.Int32`, so a wrong change that ran it from two goroutines fails on the count,
  not on a data race. Step 3 and step 4 use `t.Errorf`, so a failure in step 3 still releases Stop A and the bubble
  ends cleanly; D3 names no failure line for Stop A or the third Stop returning non-nil, so those read "first Stop =
  %v, want nil" and "third Stop = %v, want nil".

Generated checks: not used. The input is one context in three states (nil, cancelled, expired) against two calls,
and one fixed interleaving of two Stops; named examples cover every case.
