## Choices the design leaves open

1. **Times as Go marshals `time.Time`.** RFC 3339 with nanoseconds and the zone the classifier computed in (the
   server's `time.Local`), for example `2026-11-07T12:00:00.0000005-08:00`; the pin formatted `time.RFC3339`, whole
   seconds. The pin's times were in the server's local zone too (`pin:graphrag.go:1210-1213` formats the classifier's
   `time.Now()` range as it stands), and its responder converts them to UTC before use
   (`pin:processor/graph-index-temporal/query.go:173-174`). A responder decoding into `time.Time` reads both.
2. **`min_revision` is in both requests** (`MinRevisionField`, omitted when 0): the spec's "`min_revision` SHALL reach
   each derived-index read", which D4's field lists do not repeat.
3. **`limit` is a plain `int` under `limit`**, not `omitempty`, as the pin's responders read it.
4. **A reply with no `results` is refused** transient `internal` naming the subject, one error, as
   `outgoingRows`/`incomingRows` refuse a reply with no relationships: absent results would read as "nothing
   changed". A reply that does not decode or names no `producer` is `decodeIndexReply`'s, as for every index reply.
5. **Two functions, not one generic.** `globalTemporal` and `globalSpatial` share five lines of shape; a helper
   would need two callbacks.
6. **The pin carry** (`query_error_class_test.go:570-621`: a responder's classified failure surfaces with its class)
   is the `refused` subtest of each test, on the real broker, which also checks the message and that a responder's
   refusal counts no error.

## Generated checks or examples

Examples suffice: each strategy maps one classifier value onto one request and passes one reply through. The
classifier's grammar has its own tests; the outcomes here are a closed set (answer, refusal, no results, no
responder), each a named case. No new parser of external bytes in production: the new types are sent, not decoded,
here, so no fuzz target; their strict decoding is shown by the wire test.
