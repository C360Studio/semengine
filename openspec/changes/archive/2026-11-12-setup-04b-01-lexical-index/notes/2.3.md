## Choices the design leaves open

1. Only `ParseQuery` builds a `Query` outside the package; the zero `Query` still matches nothing. Every in-package
   test now goes through `ParseQuery` too: `mustSearch`, `TestScopeBeforeLimit`, `TestSearchDuringWrites` (queries
   parsed before the goroutines start), and the three properties, which write their query as text.
2. A "term" outside quotes runs to the next white space (`unicode.IsSpace`) or `"`. A quote anywhere outside a phrase
   opens one: `retry"docker compose"up` is `retry`, the phrase, `up`; `"a b"-test` excludes `test`.
3. `name:` and `body:` are matched as written (lower case), as the spec spells them: `Name:retry` is the words `name
   retry`, not refused. A quote right after the prefix counts as "more text", so `name:"retry"` is refused: it is the
   likeliest spelling of a later field-scoped phrase. Neither case is pinned in the spec; the reviewer may want the
   case question put to the architect. Only `name:"retry"` is pinned by a test.
4. The reserved check applies to an excluded term after its `-`: `-name:retry` and `-retr*` are refused (the spec
   calls the excluded term "a query term preceded by `-`").
5. `ParseQuery` dedupes plain words (Search assumes distinct words) and counts distinct words of every form while it
   reads, refusing as soon as the count passes 16. So a query that is both too long and empty, or too long with
   reserved syntax after the 17th word, is refused with `ErrQueryTooLong`. Otherwise reserved syntax is refused at
   the term, and emptiness is checked at the end. The spec orders none of these.
6. Repeated phrases or excluded terms are kept as given; they change no answer. A text holding many phrases makes each
   candidate's check longer; the request's own size bound belongs to the verb that serves it (change 3).
7. A phrase and a plain word sharing a word score that word once (`scored`), as the spec's "distinct words of the
   query's plain words and phrases" says; the model witness "word in plain and phrase scores once" checks it.

## Generated checks: decision

The query grammar has interacting forms (plain, quoted, excluded, reserved, unclosed quotes) and two stated laws, so
it gets `FuzzParseQuery`: no panic; every refusal matches one of the three errors; every parsed query has a plain word
or a phrase, distinct plain words, no empty part, at most 16 distinct words, each word a word `Tokens` gives, and it
parses back to itself when written out (`renderQuery`); and any text with its quotes removed, put in quotes, is one
phrase of `Tokens` of that text (or refused as empty or too long), never reserved. Combined forms over a history go to
`TestPropIndexMatchesModel`, whose model now filters by phrases and exclusions with its own space-joined string match.

## What makes each assertion run

- `TestPropIndexMatchesModel`: 10 named witnesses before `rapid.Check`, the 4 of task 2.2 and 6 new: a phrase held
  together, apart and across fields; two phrases with one held; an excluded word and an excluded phrase split across
  fields (limit 1); a phrase no entity holds; a word in a plain word and a phrase; only exclusions (`ErrEmptyQuery`).
  Generated: the history of task 2.2, then 1 to 4 queries of 0 to 4 plain words, 0 to 2 phrases of 1 to 3 words and
  0 to 2 excluded terms of 1 or 2 words, their parts in a generated order. Half the phrases and excluded terms are cut
  from a held field, so they often match. A scratch count (not committed) at seed 20261112, 1,000 checks: of 2,400
  queries, 193 were refused as empty, in 654 a phrase removed a scored entity, in 462 an exclusion did, and in 228 an
  entity holding every phrase was returned.
- `FuzzParseQuery`: 32 seeds, the spec's queries and refusals, each reserved form, unclosed quotes, a lone `-`, `"`,
  `-"`, `--`, 17 words and invalid UTF-8.
- `TestPropRankingIgnoresHistory`, `TestPropAbsentWordsMatchNothing`: unchanged apart from parsing their query text.
