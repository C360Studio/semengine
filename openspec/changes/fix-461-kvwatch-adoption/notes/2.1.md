# Task 2.1: `service` readKVWatch stops through `kvwatch.Stop`

## Test

`TestKVWatchFallingBehindLeavesNoWatchDelivery` in `service/message_logger_kv_watch_integration_test.go`, beside
`TestKVWatchFallingBehindEndsStream`. 600 keys (more than the stream's 100-event buffer and nats.go's 256-entry updates
buffer); the client does not write (`blockingResponseWriter`), so the reader falls behind. The test's `holdingHandler`
holds the reader's drop Warn (`KV watch buffer full, dropping event`), which is logged before the reader returns, then
waits for `natsfixture.AwaitNativeDeliveryBlocked`, then lets the Warn and the writes go. It checks the handler
returns, the stream's last event is `error`, and `natsfixture.AwaitNoStacks` over `natsfixture.NativeDeliveryStacks`.

## Choices the task left open

- **One run, not 10 cycles.** The Section 2 preamble's 10 cycles apply where one cycle's base failure is a coin flip.
  Here it is not: the held Warn keeps the reader from returning until the delivery is blocked on its full buffer, so
  every run stops a backlogged watch. The reader has read at most about 102 entries, leaving at least 498 behind a
  256-entry buffer. The base failed 4 of 4 runs below.
- **Ordering by the held log record.** The reader's drop Warn is the one point between "fell behind" and "return"
  that a test can hold without a production hook; the handler is the logger the test composes, as
  `TestKVWatchFallingBehindEndsStream` already does.
- **Context passed to `kvwatch.Stop`.** `readKVWatch`'s own `ctx` (the handler's `watchCtx`). On falling behind it is
  still live while the delivery drains. The drain ignores its cancellation: a delivery that never closes (#454) is
  bounded by `kvwatch.DrainBudget` from the stop (corrected after review; the first draft said from `cancel()`).
- Error still logged at Warn with the same message (`Failed to stop KV watcher`).
