## Choices the design leaves open

- Order in `deliver`: report, then (only when newly recorded) count one error and log it, then `maintainer.Delete`
  at the damaged revision, then `Complete`. Reporting first means a tick that publishes without the entity runs while
  Health already names it. Completing the damaged revision does not clear the record (only a higher one does).
- The log line for damage is an `Error`, once per newly damaged entity, as graph-lexical's; the maintainer's own
  refusal keeps its `Warn`.
- `refused_entity`, the `refused` outcome and the `IncompleteError` branch in `tick` stay. Every value the component
  now upserts decoded, and `graph.UnmarshalEntityState` refuses what `newContribution` refuses (a nil state, an ID the
  parser refuses, a state naming another entity), so the branch is not expected to fire; it is kept because it still
  reports truthfully if the two rules ever differ, and the maintainer's refusal is its own contract (design D2).
  Their comments now say so.
- The delete raises the marks of the groups the damaged entity would be in, as any applied delete does, so in
  `TestDamagedReplayValuePublishesTheRest` the groups `a1` and `b1` share carry `b1`'s revision.

## Follower order (open, outside this task's package)

`Follower.Complete` (`graph/follower/follower.go:347-351`) raises the watermark before `clearDamaged`. A reader that
waits on the floor alone (a test's `awaitApplied`, or a reply waiting on `min_revision`) can, for a moment, still see
a repaired entity recorded damaged. For replies that is an over-count, the safe direction (D1). For tests that assert
a count of 0 or a healthy `Health` right after a repair's floor it is a rare race: graph-lexical's
`TestSearchPastDamagedEntity` ("overwritten, then repaired") reads `Health` once after such a reply. Here the repair
checks await `Health`. Clearing before raising the watermark would close it; that is a follower change, left to the
caller.
