## Choices the design leaves open

1. Name is the first heading of the section's file (D8). A section whose heading is that first heading is never a
   question, because its heading is then in its own Name; the probes' rule is that the heading is not in the text.
   Otherwise questions follow the retrieval probe: unique heading (ignoring case), at least three distinct words
   (by `Tokens`), first 500 in shuffled order.
2. Each question goes through `ParseQuery`, as a product's would. None of the 500 is refused; one holds an
   exclusion and two hold a phrase, kept as written.
3. A question's answer is ranked among every hit (`Limit` is the corpus size), so MRR is over the full ranking, as
   the probe's was.
4. The entity ID's third segment is the section's top directory, so a behaviour question can test scope.
5. Behaviour questions keep SemSource's shape (`id`, `band`, `args.query`, `expect_*`, `twin`, `why`) and grading
   by case-insensitive substrings over hit text, and add five fields where text cannot say it: `expect_ids`,
   `expect_no_ids`, `expect_no_hits`, `expect_error`, and `expect_hits_match`, a regular expression the hit set must
   equal exactly. A phrase's expression is written from the word rules (letters and digits make words; identifiers
   split at a lower-to-upper step; plurals fold), never from the code. The decoder refuses unknown fields and a
   question with no expectation, so a misspelled field cannot make a question pass by asking nothing.
6. Every exclusion question has a twin without the exclusion that finds what the exclusion removes (X02, X04,
   X06), so the exclusion questions cannot pass vacuously. `withretry` (I01) has `"retry classified"` (P07) as twin:
   the corpus holds `RequestWithRetryClassified` in 7 sections, and P07 finds exactly them.
7. P03's first expression missed two hits that were right: `counts: [1` (the plural folds) and ``ErrorCount` +1``
   (the identifier splits). The expression was corrected to allow both; the index was not changed.
8. No statement about a declared weakness is pinned (for example, `statuses` not finding `status`): a must-pass
   question on a known gap would have to be edited when the gap is closed.

## NameWeight sweep

`TestScorecardHoldsItsFloor -v` with the constant replaced through `-overlay` (no tree edit), go1.26.11
darwin/arm64, Apple M3 Pro, 500 questions over 2,000 sections:

| NameWeight | R@1 | R@10 | MRR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (comparison only) | 25.4% | 56.0% | 0.362 |
| 2 | 24.6% | 56.0% | 0.356 |
| 3 | 23.0% | 55.6% | 0.345 |
| 5 | 22.0% | 55.6% | 0.336 |

Rule (D6): the smallest of 2, 3 and 5 within one point of the best R@10 among them. The best is 56.0% at 2, so
`NameWeight` is 2. Measured, not inferred: a heavier name weight lowers R@1 and MRR here, and weight 1 is as good
or better on every column. That is what this corpus can show: its Name is a file title shared by every section in
the file, a weak stand-in for an entity's name. The weight's benefit is unmeasured until a corpus with real entity
names is run (SemSource's runner, after change 2).

## Against the plain reference

Same 2,000 sections and 500 questions:

| Ranker | R@1 | R@10 | MRR |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `lexical`, NameWeight 2 (shipped) | 24.6% | 56.0% | 0.356 |
| `lexical`, Body only (`notes/2.4-body-only.go.txt`) | 26.0% | 55.6% | 0.365 |
| Probe's plain BM25, embedder tokens, Body only (`notes/2.4-reference.go.txt`) | 23.6% | 52.6% | 0.330 |
| Same, Name prepended to Body | 23.0% | 53.2% | 0.329 |
| `lexical` with the two sibilant plural rules removed | 24.8% | 55.8% | 0.356 |
| `lexical` with IDF fixed at 1 (the mutant below) | 23.4% | 51.6% | 0.335 |

`retrieval-probe.log`'s 38.8% R@10 is on 8,000 sections and not comparable: a larger corpus has more competitors
per question. On equal data the shipped package is 3.4 points of R@10 and 1.0 point of R@1 above the probe's plain
BM25. The reference runs as a test in `graph/embedding` (it reads the embedder's unexported tokenizer), through an
overlay, with `SC=<scorecard dir>`; add `WITHNAME=1` for the second row. The body-only row runs in
`graph/lexical` the same way with `SC=<dir> BODYONLY=1`.

## Mutants

Each through `task mutate:check` on `graph/lexical/index.go`: 3 baseline, 3 mutant and 1 after-run, `-cpu 1
-race`. The file's SHA-256 was `f627855b807250005c1f92104c3c29a56831a1ab1b5d8603af5313c08691da3c` before and
after every run. Reports were in a temporary directory on the developer's machine only.

| Wrong change | Test | Expected | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IDF is 1 | `TestScorecardHoldsItsFloor` | `scorecard_test.go:134`: `R@10 = 51.6%, below the floor of 55.0%` | detection |
| Exclusions ignored in `admits` | `TestScorecardBehaviour` | text `holds "_test.go"` (X01) | detection |
| `holdsRun` checks words, not adjacency | `TestScorecardBehaviour` | text `hits differ from the sections matching` (P01 to P07) | detection |

The behaviour mutants first ran with `-expect scorecard_test.go:263` and `:293` and came out inconclusive:
`gradeBehaviour` is a test helper, so Go reports its failures at the call site, `scorecard_test.go:233`. They were
rerun naming the text, and those reruns are the detections above.

The IDF mutant fails the floor by 3.4 points, so the floor is close to what IDF adds on this corpus (4.4 points).
With stopwords dropped, the words left vary less in how common they are, which is where IDF earns its weight.
